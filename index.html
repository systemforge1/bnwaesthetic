02 — About

Freelance since 2016. Based in Quezon City, on a plane more often than I’d like to admit.

I started with my Tatay’s Canon AE-1 and a single roll of expired Kodak Gold, photographing the fiesta on our street in Novaliches. I got maybe three usable frames. I’ve been chasing the fourth one ever since.

Nine years later the work has taken me from the rice terraces of Ifugao to a rooftop in BGC at 4am, waiting for a client’s hair and makeup to finish. What hasn’t changed is the approach: show up early, talk to people first, shoot second. Most of my favourite frames happen in the ten minutes after someone forgets I’m holding a camera.

I shoot natural light wherever the room allows it, and I edit warm — the Philippines already gives you the best golden hour on earth, so my job is mostly not to ruin it. I work solo for portraits and small editorials, and bring a second shooter plus an assistant for weddings.

Clients so far include Balangay Magazine, Kape Kalinga, Museo Bulawan, Habi Apparel, and roughly seventy couples who trusted me with a day they only get once.

04 — Process

Four steps, no surprises, no mystery invoices at the end.

  1. 01

    Kumustahan

    A free 20-minute call or a long Messenger thread — whichever you prefer. We talk about what the photos are actually for.

  2. 02

    The Plan

    You get a written quote, a mood direction, a shot list, and dates. Fifty percent reserves the slot. No hidden fees.

  3. 03

    Shoot Day

    I arrive an hour early. We start slow, we talk, and somewhere around frame forty you stop noticing the camera. That is when it gets good.

  4. 04

    Delivery

    Previews within 72 hours, the full gallery in 10–14 days. High-res downloads, print-ready files, and lifetime archive backup.

07 — FAQ

The questions that come up in almost every first message. If yours isn’t here, just ask.

Do you travel outside Metro Manila?
Constantly. Rates include anywhere inside Metro Manila; beyond that I add transport, and for anything past a four-hour drive, one night’s accommodation. Provincial and island work is a big part of what I do — Siargao, Sagada, Vigan and Bohol are all regulars. International commissions are welcome too.
How far ahead should I book?
Portraits: two to three weeks. Weddings: six to nine months, and December through February goes first because everyone wants the cool season. If your date is sooner than that, message me anyway — I deliberately keep a couple of slots open each month for short notice.
What’s the turnaround?
Previews within 72 hours, full gallery in 10–14 working days. Weddings can stretch to three weeks in peak season. You’ll get the honest date in writing before you pay anything, and I’d rather quote three weeks and deliver in two than the other way round.
Film or digital?
Both. Digital is the backbone — it’s reliable and it’s what pays the bills. But I almost always burn a roll or two of Portra alongside it, because film still handles Philippine skin tones in harsh sun better than anything I’ve calibrated. Film frames are included at no extra cost.
Can we shoot on a weekday?
Please do. Weekdays are cheaper, locations are emptier, permits are easier, and I’m not rushing to a second booking. Golden hour on a Tuesday beats golden hour on a Saturday every single time.
What happens if it rains?
This is the Philippines, so I plan for it rather than hope. Every outdoor shoot has a covered fallback location I’ve already scouted, and if the weather makes it genuinely impossible we reschedule at no charge. For what it’s worth, some of my favourite frames happened in the ten minutes right after a downpour.
How much retouching do you do?
Colour, exposure, and cleanup on distractions — always, included. I’ll take out a stray pimple, a wayward light stand, a tricycle in the background. I won’t reshape anyone’s body or change their skin tone. If that’s the brief, I’m genuinely not the right photographer for you and I’ll happily refer you to someone else.
Do you offer a wedding + prenup package?
Yes, and it works out cheaper than booking them separately. The prenup also doubles as a rehearsal — by the time the actual day arrives you’re used to me being around, which shows in the photos. Ask me for the combined rate.
